## 🧠 What this subject is about
Neural networks are computer models loosely inspired by how our brain's cells connect. By stacking many simple "neurons" in layers, computers can learn very hard things like recognising faces. This subject teaches you how these layers learn, a field called deep learning.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
Deep learning is the powerhouse behind self-driving cars, photo filters, voice assistants, and medical scanners that spot tumours. It is also the engine inside the large AI models changing the world today. For an AI and ML student, this is where the real magic gets built.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Let's build a program that recognises handwritten digits, the same idea the post office uses to read pin codes on letters. You will use a famous beginner dataset of tiny images, each showing a number from 0 to 9. Your neural network looks at the pixels and learns to say which digit it sees. At first it makes lots of mistakes, but through a process called backpropagation, it slowly corrects itself and gets smarter with each round. Watching your model go from clueless to reading numbers like a human is one of the most satisfying moments in all of computer science.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Load a dataset of small handwritten-digit images
- Build a simple network of neurons with an input, hidden, and output layer
- Train it using backpropagation so it learns from its mistakes
- Try a CNN to boost accuracy on the images

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Perceptrons
- Activation functions
- Backpropagation and training
- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)
- Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)
