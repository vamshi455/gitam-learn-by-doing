# Discrete Mathematical Structures (MATH2572)

**Category:** Faculty Core &middot; **Credits:** 4 &middot; **L-T-P-S-J:** 3-1-0-0-0

## 🧠 What this subject is about
Discrete math is the study of separate, countable things — like whole numbers, choices, and connections — instead of smooth flowing amounts. It covers logic (how to reason correctly), sets (groups of things), and graphs (dots connected by lines). It is the math that computers are built on, because computers work with distinct steps and yes/no decisions.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
Every time you use a maps app to find the shortest route, that is graph theory. Social networks use it to find your "friends of friends." Logic gates in your computer chip run on the true/false rules you learn here. Search engines, secret codes, and even how AI systems represent knowledge all lean on discrete math.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Map out your own social network as a graph. Pick 8 to 12 people you know — classmates, family, friends — and draw a dot for each person. Draw a line between two dots if those two people know each other. Now you have a real graph! Can you find who is the most "connected" person (the one with the most lines)? Can you find the shortest chain of friends between two people who do not know each other directly? This is exactly how apps suggest new friends and how "six degrees of separation" works. It makes graph theory feel personal and fun.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Draw a friendship graph with people as dots and connections as lines.
- Count each person's connections to find the most popular node.
- Trace the shortest path between two distant people.
- Try coloring the graph so no two connected people share a color (like scheduling without clashes).

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Logic and truth (true/false reasoning)
- Sets, relations, and functions
- Combinatorics (counting possibilities)
- Graph theory (nodes, edges, paths)
- Reading and writing simple proofs
