# Engineering Chemistry (CHEM1111)

**Category:** Faculty Core &middot; **Credits:** 4 &middot; **L-T-P-S-J:** 2-1-2-0-0

## 🧠 What this subject is about
Chemistry is the study of what stuff is made of and how it changes. Engineering chemistry focuses on the parts that matter for building things: how atoms join together, what makes materials strong or weak, how batteries store energy, and how plastics are made. It connects the tiny world of atoms to the real materials we use every day.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
The battery in your phone, the plastic case around it, and the metal that does not rust are all chemistry at work. Engineers choose materials based on chemistry — lightweight but strong for planes, or heat-resistant for engines. Even the cooling systems in the giant computers that train AI models rely on chemistry to move heat away safely.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Build a simple battery out of a lemon (or a potato) and light up a small LED. When you push a copper coin and a zinc nail into a lemon, a chemical reaction pushes electrons through a wire — that is electricity! Can you get enough voltage to light a tiny bulb? What happens if you connect several lemons in a row? This little experiment shows you electrochemistry in action, the same science behind every battery you own. It is safe, cheap, and genuinely surprising when the light flickers on.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Push a copper piece and a zinc piece into a lemon and connect wires.
- Measure the voltage with a cheap multimeter.
- Link several lemons in a chain to add up the voltage.
- Try to light a small LED and note what makes the current stronger or weaker.

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Atomic structure and electrons
- Chemical bonding
- Electrochemistry (how batteries work)
- Materials and their properties
- Polymers (plastics and their uses)
