# Fundamentals of Engineering Physics (PHYS1291)

**Category:** Faculty Core &middot; **Credits:** 4 &middot; **L-T-P-S-J:** 3-0-2-0-0

## 🧠 What this subject is about
Engineering physics explains the rules that everything in the universe follows — how objects move, how light and sound travel, and how electricity and magnetism work. It takes the "why" behind everyday things and turns it into clear laws you can use. Because it has a lab component, you get to test these laws with your own hands.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
Physics is why bridges stand, why your headphones make sound, and why solar panels turn sunlight into power. Cameras use optics, motors use electromagnetism, and touchscreens use modern physics. Even AI hardware — the chips that train models — depends on the physics of electrons moving through tiny circuits.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Build a simple pendulum and use it to measure the strength of gravity, right where you are standing. All you need is a string, a small weight, and a stopwatch (your phone works). Hang the weight, pull it to one side, and time how long it takes to swing back and forth. If you change the length of the string, the swing time changes in a neat, predictable way. From your measurements, you can actually calculate the value of gravity (about 9.8 meters per second squared) and see how close you get. It is a classic experiment that turns a piece of string into a scientific instrument.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Make a pendulum with a string and a small weight.
- Time how long 10 full swings take, then divide to get one swing.
- Repeat with different string lengths and record the results.
- Use the pendulum formula to calculate gravity and compare with the known value.

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Mechanics (motion, forces, energy)
- Waves and oscillations
- Optics (how light behaves)
- Electromagnetism basics
- A taste of modern physics
