# Foundations of Electrical and Electronics Engineering (24EECE2231)

**Category:** Faculty Core &middot; **Credits:** 4 &middot; **L-T-P-S-J:** 3-0-2-0-0

## 🧠 What this subject is about
This subject teaches you how electricity flows and how we control it to make useful gadgets. You learn about circuits (the paths electricity travels), the simple rules that govern them like Ohm's law, and the tiny parts — diodes and transistors — that switch and steer current. It is the foundation for understanding every electronic device around you.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
Everything with a battery or a plug runs on these ideas: lights, phones, robots, and cars. Transistors, which you study here, are the building blocks of computer chips — a modern chip has billions of them. Since AI runs on powerful chips, understanding electronics helps you see how the hardware behind machine learning actually works.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Build a small LED night light that turns on by itself when the room gets dark. Using a light sensor, a transistor as a switch, a resistor, and an LED, you can make a circuit that senses darkness and lights up automatically. As you build it, you will use Ohm's law to pick the right resistor so the LED does not burn out, and you will see a transistor act like a tiny automatic switch. It is a real, useful gadget you can keep by your bed, and it teaches the core ideas of electronics in a hands-on way.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Wire up an LED with a resistor and use Ohm's law to choose the resistor value.
- Add a light-sensing part that reacts to darkness.
- Use a transistor as a switch to turn the LED on and off automatically.
- Test it in bright and dark rooms and adjust the circuit.

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Basic circuits and how current flows
- Ohm's law (voltage, current, resistance)
- Kirchhoff's laws for circuits
- Diodes and how they steer current
- Transistors as switches and amplifiers
