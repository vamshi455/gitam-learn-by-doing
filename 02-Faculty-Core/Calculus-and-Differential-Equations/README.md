# Calculus and Differential Equations (MATH1341)

**Category:** Faculty Core &middot; **Credits:** 4 &middot; **L-T-P-S-J:** 3-1-0-0-0

## 🧠 What this subject is about
Calculus is the math of things that change. It gives you tools to measure how fast something is changing (that's a derivative) and how much of something adds up over time (that's an integral). Differential equations go one step further: they describe rules for how a thing changes, so you can predict its future. Together, they are the language for anything that moves, grows, or shrinks.

## 🌍 Why it matters in the real world
Your phone uses calculus to smooth out shaky video. Weather apps solve differential equations to guess tomorrow's rain. In AI and machine learning, calculus is how a model *learns*: it uses derivatives (called gradients) to slowly get better at its job, step by step. Even a game character falling and bouncing is running calculus behind the scenes.

## 🎯 A practical real-world problem to learn it
Imagine you want to predict how a cup of hot chocolate cools down on your table. You know it starts hot and slowly reaches room temperature, but it cools fast at first and slower later. Can you build a little model that predicts the temperature at any minute? This is a famous rule called Newton's Law of Cooling, and it is a differential equation you can actually test at home. Grab a thermometer, measure the temperature every minute, and see if your math matches reality. It is a small project, but it teaches you rates of change, derivatives, and how to solve a real differential equation.

## 🛠️ What you'd build or try
- Measure a hot drink's temperature every minute for 20 minutes and write it in a table.
- Draw a graph of temperature vs. time and notice how the *slope* (rate of cooling) changes.
- Learn the cooling equation and use it to predict the temperature curve.
- Compare your prediction to your real measurements and tweak the numbers until they match.

## 📚 Key ideas you'll practice
- Rates of change and what a derivative really means
- Limits as the foundation of calculus
- Integrals and adding up small changes
- Setting up and solving a first-order differential equation
- Using derivatives for optimization (finding fastest/slowest points)
